# Medsera Arabic glossary

One concept = one word, in marketing, docs and every app screen. The member app is the reference; staff and admin copy follow it. Built from about 10,000 English/Arabic UI strings in the Medsera apps (Nov 2026), checked against YouTube, Google Play and LinkedIn Arabic help, and platform conventions (Udemy, Telegram, WhatsApp, Facebook groups). Medsera-specific terms are marked as such; the rest applies to any Arabic learning or community product.

**Brand:** ميدسيرا, treated as feminine («ميدسيرا تجمع»، «منصة ميدسيرا»). In Latin contexts: Medsera.

## Rules for English inside Arabic

1. **Keep in Latin**: exam names, subtests and acronyms — AMC, OET, MCQ, AHPRA, PLAB, IELTS, Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking, Part 1, Grade B, recalls. Don't translate OET subtests (today «محادثة» means both Speaking and chat — confusing).
2. **Arabic frame around the Latin token**: «امتحان AMC»، «قسم Writing في OET»، «أسئلة MCQ».
3. **Nothing glued to Latin**: no «الـOET»، «بالـAMC»، «OETك». Write «مجتمع OET الخاص بك» or rephrase.
4. **Line edges**: don't start a line or bullet with a Latin token if punctuation can flip; put an Arabic word first. In HTML wrap mixed runs in `<bdi>`.
5. **First-mention parentheses** only for terms doctors know in English (Tutor, Mock, Qbank, rubric). Never on buttons, tabs or notifications, never for everyday words.
6. **Product names** stay Latin with no Arabic article: Stripe, YouTube, WhatsApp may also be written واتساب، يوتيوب in marketing (common Arabic forms).
